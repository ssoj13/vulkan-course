## Who this is for

You know graphics from the artist's or tools side: Maya, Houdini, shaders, maybe some OpenGL. Vulkan feels like a
wall of structs. This course takes it apart one brick at a time, and every new idea is tied to something you already
know from CG.

## How the pieces stack up

Everything in the course sits on one of four layers, and every lesson starts by saying which:

- **Concept** - ideas such as command buffers, queues and explicit APIs, true for any modern graphics API.
- **Vulkan API** - the objects you create and the calls you make.
- **Loader & driver** - the software between your calls and the chip: the Vulkan loader, layers, the vendor driver.
- **Hardware** - the GPU itself: shader cores, VRAM, the command processor and its engines.

The first section of the course walks this chain from the bottom up, one link per lesson:

`GPU → driver → loader → VkInstance → VkPhysicalDevice → VkDevice → VkQueue`

Each link is built on the one before it: the loader finds the drivers, the instance is your connection to the loader,
GPUs are found through the instance, a device is opened on one GPU, and queues live inside the device. Everything
later - windows, pipelines, memory, synchronization - hangs off the device and its queues.
