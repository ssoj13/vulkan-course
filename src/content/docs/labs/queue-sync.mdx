The GPU runs your work later, on several queues at once, and nothing orders that work except what you say. Vulkan
gives you three tools for saying it, one per kind of edge. A **fence** is GPU → CPU: the host waits on it
(`vkWaitForFences`) to learn that a submission finished. A **semaphore** is GPU → GPU between submissions, usually
across queues: one batch signals it, another waits on it. A *binary* semaphore is a one-shot token, one signal for one
wait. A *timeline* semaphore is a counter that only goes up, and a wait asks for "value ≥ N". A **pipeline barrier**
orders commands inside one queue. Barriers are not modeled here; they come in the synchronization module.

Submission order on one queue is the *logical* order that barriers and semaphores refer to
([how a program talks to the GPU](lesson:talking-to-gpu#command-buffers-and-queues)). By itself it does not make later
work wait for earlier work: batches may overlap and finish in any order. Between different queues there is no order at
all ([queue families](lesson:queue-families#underneath)). The rules this lab follows are the spec's own. A semaphore
signal covers its batch and everything submitted earlier on that queue. A wait holds its batch and everything submitted
later on that queue, but only from the stages in its `stageMask` onward. A fence covers its submission and everything
earlier on the queue.

How to use it: pick a scenario and scrub through time. Lanes are the CPU and the real graphics, async compute and
transfer queues of the course GPU (families 0, 2 and 1). Hatched bars are stalls, arrows go from a signal to the wait
it satisfies, and red marks point at problems. Below the chart, edit the host program: add or remove waits and
signals, change a stage mask or a timeline value, switch a semaphore between binary and timeline, or move a call up or
down. Every problem is found from the rules, not from the drawn run. A race is a race even when the bars happen not
to overlap. Valid-usage messages quote the real VUID text.

What is simplified: the drawing is one schedule the spec allows, not the only one. Durations are illustrative units.
Each `vkQueueSubmit2` carries one batch, and signals use stage mask `ALL_COMMANDS`. Shared resources are assumed to be
created with `VK_SHARING_MODE_CONCURRENT`, so no queue family ownership transfer is needed, and mapped memory is
assumed host-coherent. In *Frames in flight* two frames overlap on the graphics queue because nothing here orders
them; a real renderer orders writes to its shared color and depth images with barriers and the swapchain's semaphores.

Things to try:

- in *Upload, then render*, change the wait's stage to `COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OUTPUT`: the fragment shader reads the texture
  before the copy ends; change it to `VERTEX_SHADER` and the whole draw waits;
- move the render call above the upload: a binary wait without a submitted signal; then make `uploaded` a timeline
  semaphore and see that waiting before the signal is allowed;
- in *Async compute + timeline*, remove the wait of the second simulation: it now overwrites particles that the draw
  is still reading;
- in *Frames in flight*, remove the second `vkWaitForFences` on `F0`, or uncheck "created signaled".
