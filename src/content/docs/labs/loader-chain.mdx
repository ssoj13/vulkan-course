Your program never calls the driver directly: every Vulkan call goes through the loader and the layers it inserted.
This lab follows one call hop by hop on the machine the course was built on - its eight registered implicit layers,
the validation layer step 02 enables, and its NVIDIA driver. The background is in
[the loader lesson](lesson:loader-and-instance#layers-implicit-and-explicit) and its
[Underneath section](lesson:loader-and-instance#underneath).

Pick a call, then step or play the animation; each hop has a caption. Below the chain is the table of implicit layers
with their real enable and disable variables. The layer order is the one the loader itself printed for step 02:
implicit layers closest to the program, the layers the program names after them.

What to notice: the call's level decides its path. Instance calls end in the loader's terminator, which calls every
driver; a physical-device call ends there too, but only its own driver is called; a device call such as
`vkQueueSubmit` has no terminator at all, and through a pointer from `vkGetDeviceProcAddr` it does not even enter the
loader.

Things to try:

- add the second driver and compare `vkEnumeratePhysicalDevices` with `vkGetPhysicalDeviceProperties`;
- switch `vkQueueSubmit` between the loader export and `vkGetDeviceProcAddr`;
- set `ENABLE_VULKAN_RENDERDOC_CAPTURE` and see RenderDoc join the chain; then set its disable variable too;
- set `DISABLE_LAYER_NV_OPTIMUS_1`, and turn the validation layer off, until no layer is left.

The messages these layers print are the subject of the next lab, [Debug messenger filters](lab:messenger-filter).
